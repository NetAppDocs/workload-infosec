Option 1: 

Information security is a core part of NetApp Workload Factory design and operation. This documentation set is designed to help security and cloud platform teams review Workload Factory and complete onboarding with a clear, auditable sequence.

Workload Factory is a SaaS lifecycle management platform designed to help users optimize workloads (such as storage operations, VMware-related workflows, EDA projects, and database environments) using Amazon FSx for NetApp ONTAP.

== What security teams typically need to approve
Security review and onboarding usually focuses on four questions:

. *Shared responsibility boundaries*
Where NetApp responsibility ends and where customer configuration begins.

. *Authentication and authorization controls*
How Workload Factory accesses customer AWS accounts, how permissions are scoped, and how least privilege is enforced.

. *Data handling, residency, and privacy*
What operational data classes exist, where they are stored, how long they are retained, and what information may leave the customer environment.

. *Auditability and monitoring*
What can be monitored (audit records, metrics, telemetry), how long it is retained, and what evidence can be collected during onboarding.

== What you can do during onboarding to speed approval
* Start with Tier 1 (read-only) permissions and expand only as required.
* Use AWS CloudTrail to validate sts:AssumeRole activity for the integration role.
* Decide upfront whether you need ONTAP-native operations (which may require deploying Lambda link or NetApp Console agent).
* Decide upfront whether generative AI is permitted and whether residency constraints require single-region inference profiles.

== What Workload Factory does (security context)

NetApp Workload Factory is a SaaS life-cycle management platform designed to help you optimize and manage workloads (for example, storage, VMware migrations, EDA projects, and database environments) using Amazon FSx for NetApp ONTAP.
Your workloads run in AWS, and Workload Factory interacts with AWS and (when required) ONTAP management endpoints through well-defined credential and connectivity paths.

== Core security principles used in this guide

Confidentiality::
Protect data from unauthorized access through identity controls, least privilege, encryption, and network boundaries.

Integrity::
Protect systems and configurations from unauthorized or unintended change using permission scoping, change control, and auditability.

Availability::
Ensure authorized users can access the service and workloads through resilient AWS designs and operational monitoring.

== What security teams typically need to understand

Shared responsibility boundaries::
Where NetApp and AWS responsibilities end and where customer configuration begins.

Authentication and authorization controls::
How Workload Factory authenticates into your AWS account and how you scope permissions.

Data handling and privacy::
What data categories exist (metadata, logs, telemetry), where they are stored, how long they are retained, and what may leave the VPC.

Auditability and monitoring::
What signals are available to support detection, troubleshooting, and investigations.
